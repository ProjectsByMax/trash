Calling it from the photo

You've already decided to appeal. These are the grounds a photo can give you — every one that applies goes in the row, strongest first.

  1. 1

    Can you read the label on the bin?

    The address painted on the container itself — not the house behind it, not a street sign. A background address that disagrees with the bin means nothing; the bin governs.

    No label, or unreadable → Can't see the label. Holds no matter how full the bin is.

  2. 2

    Is there sun glare across the bin?

    Shooting into low sun, veiling flare over the load, blown highlights where the evidence should be.

    Yes → Sun glare.

  3. 3

    Is the picture blurry?

    Soft enough that you can't make out where material ends and background begins — motion blur, a dirty lens, a frame grabbed from video.

    Yes → Blurry photo.

  4. 4

    Does it clear Republic's own threshold?

    Straight off their training slide: an Extra Yard needs material above the lip across more than 50% of the visible area, or, where the view is limited, at least 1 foot above the rim. A front-load bin runs about four feet tall, so a foot is roughly a quarter of its visible height.

    Short of either → Under 50% above the lip or Under 1 foot above the rim. Their own numbers, used against them.

  5. 5

    Is the trash above the rim at all?

    Two things prove it, and nothing else does: the load breaks the silhouette — outlined against a wall, the sky or the street, with none of the bin behind it — or it physically lifts the lids off the rim so they ride on the load instead of sitting flat.

    Neither → Not over the lid, or Lid barely popping up when the lids are only cracked.

  6. 6

    Where does the geotag actually point?

    Cab-camera frames carry latitude and longitude in the overlay. The app resolves them against LA County's address data and compares to your rent roll, exact house number.

    Not an exact match → GPS discrepancy, and the row is marked No.

    Expect the counter that the bin sits in an alley behind the building — decide which of these you want to push.

Looking down into a bin is not the same as looking at a full one. The truck camera sits about ten feet up and looks down through the opening, so material sitting well below the far wall still projects above the near rim in the picture. That one illusion is what makes half-full bins get billed. If you can trace the bin's far wall behind the trash, the trash is inside the bin — however high it looks.

You do three things: drop the photo, tick Submitted once you've filed it, set the Result when Republic answers. Date, bin label, GPS and every appeal ground are read off the photo.
